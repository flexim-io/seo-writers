# Visual storytelling template library

Canonical reference for `visual-storytelling`. Use it to select semantic composition. Never copy a product, wording, or geometry from an example without checking the reader question.

## Universal template principle

- Stable template logic: reader question, visual relationship, hierarchy, and composition.
- Variable content: product, audience, data, names, interfaces, copy, and brand.
- Flexim, Topic Suggestions, and finished articles are examples, not template definitions.
- Never put a domain example into the master template name.

## Statuses

- ✅ `ready`: master has been built and tested on real product material.
- ○ `planned`: template is defined but no master has been built yet.

## 1. Editorial and covers

### 1.1. Image-led heroes

- ✅ **Editorial hero**: expressive raster image, one strong visual idea, minimal branding.
- ○ **Conceptual metaphor**: a physical object or scene explains an abstract idea.
- ○ **Object as idea**: one object symbolizes the problem or solution.
- ○ **Lone operator**: a person or workstation inside a larger system or space.
- ○ **Impossible object**: a familiar object behaves in an unexpected way.
- ○ **Signal in noise**: one meaningful element appears inside visual noise.

### 1.2. Type-led heroes

- ○ **Thesis poster**: one strong idea in large type.
- ○ **Number hero**: the main number becomes the visual center.
- ○ **Question cover**: the composition revolves around the buyer's question.
- ○ **Quote card**: a short statement with source and context.
- ○ **Definition card**: a new term and concise explanation.

### 1.3. Contrast-led heroes

- ○ **Before / after cover**: two system states.
- ○ **One vs many**: one controlled process against many fragmented tools.
- ○ **Signal vs noise**: useful information separated from irrelevant material.
- ○ **Manual vs automated**: two ways to perform one job.
- ○ **Fragmented vs connected**: disconnected objects become a system.

### 1.4. Diagrammatic heroes

- ✅ **Interface metaphor hero with floating actions**: abstract interfaces or devices frame 2–4 large thesis statements.
- ○ **Object pair hero**: two objects represent two sides of the story.
- ○ **Floating labels hero**: large labels around one object reveal the idea.
- ○ **Mini workflow hero**: a short process becomes a cover composition.
- ○ **Tool constellation hero**: several tools gather around one job.
- ○ **Central object with callouts**: callouts explain one central symbol.
- ○ **Input / output hero**: two states show transformation.
- ○ **Human and AI collaboration hero**: human and AI have distinct roles in one job.

## 2. Commercial choice

### 2.1. Decision routing

- ✅ **Buying decision tree**: the buyer's main question routes to a recommended product type.
- ○ **Constraint router**: choice depends on budget, team, data, or task frequency.
- ○ **Use-case selector**: select a tool by the exact job.
- ○ **Starting-point selector**: recommendation depends on current state.
- ○ **Plan selector**: select a plan by volume and complexity.
- ○ **Escalation tree**: show when a basic solution is no longer sufficient.

### 2.2. Product comparison

- ○ **Job coverage matrix**: jobs covered by each product.
- ○ **Capability matrix**: concrete capabilities side by side.
- ○ **Trade-off matrix**: benefits shown with limits.
- ○ **Category comparison**: compare solution types rather than brands.
- ○ **Best-fit comparison**: identify the audience for each option.
- ○ **Overlap map**: reveal duplicated product jobs.
- ○ **Gap map**: show a job no current-stack component covers.

### 2.3. Economics

- ○ **Subscription stack**: subscriptions that make up process cost.
- ○ **Cost per outcome**: cost tied to outcome rather than feature.
- ○ **Build / buy / ignore**: three economic strategies.
- ○ **Tool replacement map**: costs removed by a new product.
- ○ **Budget allocation**: distribution of a limited budget.
- ○ **Frequency threshold**: task repetition that justifies a dedicated tool.

## 3. Processes and workflows

### 3.1. Ownership

- ✅ **Workflow comparison**: compare several solutions across the same workflow stages.
- ○ **Swimlane workflow**: every participant has a separate lane.
- ○ **Handoff chain**: pass an artifact between people and tools.
- ○ **Responsibility map**: separate decision, execution, and review ownership.
- ○ **Human / AI split**: divide responsibility between a person and AI.
- ○ **Tool ownership map**: identify the tool controlling each stage.

### 3.2. Sequence

- ○ **Linear workflow**: input-to-outcome sequence.
- ○ **Stage-gate process**: checks and decisions between stages.
- ○ **State progression**: an object moves through explicit states.
- ○ **Timeline workflow**: stages tied to time.
- ○ **Input / transformation / output**: simple three-part system.
- ○ **Failure path**: where and why the process breaks.

### 3.3. Repeating systems

- ○ **Feedback loop**: an outcome returns as a new signal.
- ○ **Operating cycle**: repeating weekly or monthly work.
- ○ **Flywheel**: cumulative effect of several actions.
- ○ **Optimization loop**: measure, change, and retest.
- ○ **Improvement loop**: launch, observe, analyze, and update.
- ○ **Bottleneck loop**: one recurring limit slows the entire system.

## 4. Research and evidence

### 4.1. Evidence aggregation

- ✅ **Evidence funnel**: several sources converge into one research process and result.
- ○ **Evidence stack**: sources arranged in layers.
- ○ **Source-to-claim map**: each claim linked to support.
- ○ **Signal convergence**: independent signals support one conclusion.
- ○ **Weighted evidence**: sources carry different importance.
- ○ **Research pipeline**: collect, filter, evaluate, and apply information.

### 4.2. Evaluation

- ○ **Proof ladder**: move from weak signal to strong evidence.
- ○ **Confidence scale**: confidence in a conclusion.
- ○ **Claim / evidence matrix**: match claims with supporting evidence.
- ○ **Hypothesis test**: hypothesis, observation, criterion, and result.
- ○ **Decision threshold**: evidence sufficient for action.
- ○ **Evidence scorecard**: several criteria form a conclusion without implying a universal truth score.

### 4.3. Uncertainty

- ○ **Known / unknown map**: known, assumed, and missing information.
- ○ **Missing evidence map**: data still required.
- ○ **Risk matrix**: error likelihood and impact.
- ○ **Assumption stack**: assumptions supporting a decision.
- ○ **False confidence map**: precise values that overstate reliability.
- ○ **Evidence gaps**: breaks between data and conclusion.

## 5. Object and system anatomy

### 5.1. Documents and records

- ✅ **Annotated record**: explain one real or demo object through fields, relations, and next action.
- ○ **Specification anatomy**: components of a useful brief or request.
- ○ **Record anatomy**: data, metadata, status, and relations.
- ○ **Result item anatomy**: fields in a result or found-object card.
- ○ **Prompt anatomy**: instruction, context, constraints, and output.
- ○ **Opportunity card anatomy**: problem, audience, value, effort, and next action.

### 5.2. Systems

- ○ **Exploded system**: separate a system into independent parts.
- ○ **Layered stack**: show data, logic, interface, and outcome as layers.
- ○ **Context bundle**: data passed to AI.
- ○ **MCP connection map**: context exchange between apps and AI.
- ○ **System boundary**: inside and outside the product.
- ○ **Input / output anatomy**: what the system receives and returns.

### 5.3. Relationships

- ○ **Entity relationship map**: relations among products, users, objects, and categories.
- ○ **Hub-and-spoke**: one central object serving several consumers.
- ○ **Dependency tree**: what depends on what.
- ○ **Ecosystem map**: a product inside a wider tool system.
- ○ **Context graph**: information contributing to a decision.
- ○ **Ownership graph**: who owns an object at each stage.

## 6. Change and results

### 6.1. Change over time

- ○ **Timeline**: chronological events.
- ○ **Progression curve**: metric change over time.
- ○ **Maturity ladder**: movement from basic to advanced practice.
- ○ **Release evolution**: product change across releases.
- ○ **Adoption curve**: spread of a new practice.
- ○ **Compounding effect**: accumulated result from sequential actions.

### 6.2. Baseline comparison

- ○ **Before / after system**: workflow structure before and after change.
- ○ **Delta chart**: improvement relative to baseline.
- ○ **Benchmark comparison**: result against a market or target.
- ○ **Conversion waterfall**: where outcome is lost between stages.
- ○ **Scenario comparison**: optimistic, baseline, and conservative cases.
- ○ **Expected vs actual**: expectation compared with observed result.

### 6.3. Capacity and allocation

- ○ **Effort allocation**: where team time goes.
- ○ **Budget allocation**: where budget goes.
- ○ **Workload capacity**: how many objects a system can process.
- ○ **Automation coverage**: portion of a workflow automated.
- ○ **Time saved**: stages that became shorter.
- ○ **Bottleneck chart**: stage limiting overall outcome.

## 7. Instructions and application

### 7.1. Step-by-step

- ○ **Numbered sequence**: short action sequence.
- ○ **Checklist**: checks before completion.
- ○ **Recipe card**: inputs, actions, and outcome.
- ○ **Setup map**: connect tools.
- ○ **Command-to-outcome**: command or prompt and result.
- ○ **Do / avoid**: correct and incorrect approaches.

### 7.2. Interface storytelling

- ✅ **Branded product UI showcase**: one complete current product screen in a clean branded composition without annotations.
- ○ **Focused feature screenshot**: crop around one feature or action.
- ○ **Contextual UI screenshot**: interface inside the user's working scenario.
- ○ **UI proof pair**: two real screens prove a transition, action, or result.
- ○ **Responsive UI set**: one feature on desktop and mobile.
- ○ **Annotated screenshot**: interface with a few meaningful callouts.
- ○ **UI walkthrough**: sequence of screens.
- ○ **State comparison**: one screen in different states.
- ○ **Zoomed detail**: full interface and enlarged critical detail.
- ○ **Action path**: where to click and what happens.
- ○ **Input / output screenshot pair**: action and result.

### 7.3. Operational templates

- ○ **Pre-release checklist**: checks before publication, launch, or handoff.
- ○ **Weekly operating rhythm**: repeatable work by day or stage.
- ○ **Runbook**: standard-situation actions.
- ○ **Escalation path**: when and to whom a problem moves.
- ○ **Review protocol**: result-review sequence.
- ○ **Definition of done**: completion conditions.

## 8. Discovery, competition, and portfolio

### 8.1. Opportunity discovery

- ○ **Signal cluster map**: group scattered signals around opportunities or problems.
- ○ **Opportunity landscape**: place opportunities by value and feasibility.
- ○ **Gap map**: unmet needs or absent solutions.
- ○ **Intent ladder**: movement from problem awareness to choice and action.
- ○ **Relevance map**: relationship between opportunity, product, and audience.
- ○ **Qualification funnel**: narrow a large set to an approved next action.

### 8.2. Market and competition

- ○ **Market composition**: categories of solutions in a market or space.
- ○ **Competitor coverage map**: needs competitors already cover.
- ○ **Feasibility map**: potential value against implementation difficulty.
- ○ **Solution-type map**: group solution formats by job.
- ○ **Weakness map**: places existing solutions can improve.
- ○ **Market ownership map**: participants by segment or position.

### 8.3. Portfolio management

- ○ **Opportunity-to-funnel map**: opportunities by journey stage.
- ○ **Portfolio calendar**: initiatives or content over time.
- ○ **Relationship map**: portfolio object relations.
- ○ **Overlap map**: duplicated initiatives, features, or content.
- ○ **Coverage matrix**: audiences and problems matched to solutions.
- ○ **Lifecycle map**: idea, selected, in progress, released, and update.

### 8.4. AI-enabled operations

- ○ **Skills vs agents**: repeatable instruction compared with an exploratory or executing system.
- ○ **Prompt vs context**: instruction content separated from supplied data.
- ○ **Human review checkpoints**: decisions requiring a person.
- ○ **AI toolchain**: roles of different AI tools.
- ○ **Context handoff**: structured input moving through AI into an outcome.
- ○ **Quality control map**: accuracy, usefulness, requirement, and product checks.

## Quick template selection

| Reader question | Suitable families |
| --- | --- |
| What is this product or article and why is it interesting? | Editorial hero, diagrammatic hero, conceptual metaphor |
| Which product or approach should I choose? | Buying decision tree, comparison matrix, constraint router |
| Who performs each stage? | Workflow comparison, swimlane workflow, responsibility map |
| How is this object or document structured? | Annotated record, anatomy, exploded system |
| How was this conclusion reached? | Evidence funnel, source-to-claim map, proof ladder |
| What changed? | Before / after system, delta chart, timeline |
| How do I reproduce this? | Numbered sequence, annotated screenshot, checklist |
| How do I find the next product opportunity? | Opportunity landscape, gap map, qualification funnel |

## Master template fields

For every new master, record:

1. `name`: stable template name.
2. `category`: category and subcategory.
3. `readerQuestion`: reader question.
4. `storyRole`: hook, proof, explain, compare, orient, demonstrate, or summarize.
5. `layoutLogic`: stable composition logic.
6. `variableContent`: replaceable data and text.
7. `dataRequirements`: real, demo, or illustrative data.
8. `aspectRatios`: supported formats.
9. `mobileBehavior`: what remains readable at small size.
10. `sourceFile`: editable master.
11. `example`: finished application.
12. `status`: ready, draft, or planned.
